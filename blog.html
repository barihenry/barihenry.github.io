I am firmly of the opinion that our colloquial syntax in physics matters. What we call things matters. It's one thing to be able to do calculations computer-like and spit out the correct answer in terms of numbers or equations every time. It's another thing entirely to be able to intuit what the answer is without needing to do that kind of explicit computation. Intuition is important yet it's not explicitly taught, too often. Not that I necessarily blame instructors for failing to do so. But it certainly doesn't help that some physics terms are misnomers, either subtly or severely. The one that comes to mind right now is shielding. A (positive) test charge placed in the environment of a plasma (i.e. free ions and electrons) will naturally attract electrons that will affect the total flux (and therefore the apparent field to an outside observer). Naturally it will repel free ions, but it's not entirely clear what effect this has. We also call this "shielding"... I def need to deliberate more.
